Demand

–

How much electricity Britain is using, including power from small wind and solar farms on local networks. It is everything being generated, plus imports, minus exports and power going into storage.

Last 24 hours

Carbon intensity

–

Grams of CO₂ emitted for every kilowatt-hour of electricity. Lower is cleaner. This is NESO's official half-hourly figure, and the very low to very high rating is NESO's own scale.

If NESO's figure is unavailable, the dashboard estimates it from the live mix using NESO's published emission factors.

Last 24 hours

Wholesale price

–

The spot price on the APX/EPEX market for the latest half hour, from Elexon's market index data. It is the bulk price of electricity, not your household rate: bills add network costs, levies and VAT, and fixed tariffs don't follow it.

It can go negative when there is more wind and solar than the grid can use.

Grid frequency

–

The grid runs at 50 Hz. When demand runs ahead of supply the frequency dips slightly; when supply runs ahead it rises. NESO keeps it between 49.8 and 50.2 Hz in normal operation, and the legal limits are 49.5 to 50.5 Hz.

Readings are 15 seconds apart. The line shows the last hour, scaled to the normal band. More on frequency, including inertia, the response NESO holds and recent dips.

    Cleanest times to use electricity

    Carbon intensity for the past day and NESO's forecast for the next two days. Using electricity in a cleaner window, for example charging a car or running the washing machine, means less of it comes from gas.

      Records and history

      Forecasts published by Elexon and NESO. Demand is Elexon's national demand forecast: what the transmission network has to supply, so it falls when the sun comes out on rooftops and small solar farms. When the latest forecast leaves national demand out, the chart shows transmission demand, which also includes power stations' own use and pumping. Wind is the forecast for large, metered wind farms. Solar is NESO's forecast for solar panels on local networks. Carbon intensity is NESO's forecast for Great Britain.

      Where it's coming from

      Large power stations report their output to Elexon every five minutes. Smaller wind and solar farms connect to local networks and aren't metered in real time, so their share is an estimate. Select a source for more detail.

      Imports, exports and storage

      Undersea cables, called interconnectors, link Britain to its neighbours. Power usually flows towards whichever market is more expensive at the time. Each bar is scaled to the cables' total capacity, so a full bar means the cables are running flat out.

      Storage soaks up spare power when it's cheap and returns it later. Storage bars use a fixed 3 GW scale, roughly the size of Britain's pumped hydro fleet. Elexon only reports battery discharging, not charging.

      Each neighbour’s carbon intensity is live for France, from RTE (its generation), and Denmark, from Energinet (electricity used in western Denmark, where Viking Link lands), every 15 minutes. With an ENTSO-E key (see the README), the others are worked out from each country’s generation mix as reported to ENTSO-E, on NESO’s factors; without one, or when a figure is late, they use NESO’s typical figures, and Norway, almost all hydro, an estimate. Britain’s own figure counts only what is generated here, on NESO’s factors.
