    How this works

    NESO keeps a register of every agreement to use the transmission network to export power: its Transmission Entry Capacity. Each project has a row with the capacity it has connected now, the change it has agreed (for a new project, all of it), the date that change takes effect, and its stage: scoping, awaiting consents, consents approved, under construction or commissioning, or built. Projects on distribution networks big enough to affect the transmission network have “embedded” agreements and are included.

    The dashboard reads the whole register when NESO publishes a new version, about twice a week, and checks for one every six hours. Projects are grouped by the plant types NESO lists: storage alongside solar or wind counts with the solar or wind, and two or more kinds of generation at one site count as mixed. Capacity is what the project may export, in megawatts; a battery’s is its power, not how long it can run.

    The queue is far bigger than Britain needs: many projects will never be built, and some dates have already passed. Scoping projects are the least certain. Dates are the ones in the register, which often move.
