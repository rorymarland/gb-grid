Grid frequency

How closely supply matches demand, second by second, and what keeps it there.

Right now

Every generator on the grid spins in step at 50 times a second. When demand runs ahead of supply, the difference comes out of the energy stored in those spinning machines and they slow down, so the frequency falls; when supply runs ahead, it rises. NESO keeps it within 0.2 Hz of 50 Hz in normal running and must never let it pass 49.5 or 50.5 Hz. Readings are Elexon’s, every 15 seconds.

How steady

Today so far, and how the last 24 hours of readings spread around 50 Hz.

Inertia

NESO’s estimate of the energy stored in the grid’s spinning machines, in gigavolt-ampere seconds. More inertia means frequency falls more slowly after a trip, giving response services time to act.

Response held now

What NESO bought in its daily auction for this four-hour block. Low services push power in when frequency falls; high services take it out when it rises.

Frequency events

Sudden falls and rises in the last week, usually a power station or interconnector tripping. At 15-second resolution the true lowest point, reached within seconds, is usually missed, so these figures understate how far it went.
