        How regional carbon intensity works

        NESO estimates the carbon intensity of the electricity used in each of the 14 regions run by the local network companies. It follows power as it flows between regions, so a region is credited with the mix that actually reaches it, not just what’s generated there.

        That’s why the Scottish regions often sit at or near 0 g/kWh for days: they generate far more wind, nuclear and hydro power than they use, and the surplus flows south. Southern England, which relies more on gas and imports, is usually the most carbon-intensive.

        Past values are NESO’s estimates for each half hour, and future values are its forecast, which is updated every half hour. The solar layer shows Sheffield Solar’s estimate of solar output in each region, which is only available for the past and the present.
